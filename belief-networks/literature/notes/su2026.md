**p. 1.** … al., 2025): Datasets are described as “diverse” without specifying what dimension (e.g., stylistic vs. semantic) and component (e.g., variety vs. balance in Fig. 1) of diversity is meant (Zhao et al., 2024; Shaib et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2024; Nguyen and Ploeger, 2025). Outside NLP, diversity has long been decomposed into three distinct components (Fig. 1, see also §4.1)— **variety (how many categories the data spans), balance (how evenly data are distributed across them), and disparity (how different the categories are from one another**) (Stirling, 2007). These components have more recently been used in NLP, for example, to theoretically categorize measures into what components they are likely to be sensitive to (Estève et al., 2025).
