# Notes: Dialogues Between Humans and an Artificial Belief System

Colby, Kenneth Mark; Smith, David Canfield (1969). IJCAI 1969. https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/1969/32

3 marks in 2 passages, in page order. Page numbers are those of the PDF. Marked words are in bold; the text around them is the sentence before and the sentence after.

**p. 1.** Instead of placing a person under the artificial conditions of a laboratory experiment, we can subject an artificial system to the naturally-occurring conditions of human linguistic communication. **Our purpose is to study the processes of credibility functions in a synthesized artifact designed to perform the intelligent task of arriving at states of belief** **or disbelief about what it is told by persons in dialogues**. Some parallels between these processes in an artificial belief system and in human credibility development will be apparent in later sections.

**p. 2.** We shall describe what happens to each type of input during the conversational phase termed ‘Talktime’. **A statement is characterized by its format and by a period as its terminal symbol.** Upon entry a statement is put on the statement list of the current informant engaging in the dialogue.
